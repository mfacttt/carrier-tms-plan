What the two weeks produced

Not an estimate. A decomposition.

Phase 1 · 5 directions · by mid-November

Foundation and the dispatcher's day

What you get
  • Your company set up once — entities, logins, who sees what, how pay is calculated.
  • All your data in one place — customers, trucks, trailers, drivers, warehouses.
  • Loads from a broker's offer to "delivered", with profit on each.
  • The weekly board — drag a load onto a driver.
  • The map — where everything is right now.
The problem today

Your dispatch lives inside someone else's system, on their terms and their price. Your operating data — every load, rate and lane you ever ran — sits in their database.

After this phase it is yours, and the history you build is an asset instead of a hostage.

Where we go further
  • Profit the moment a load is entered, not at month end.
  • Driver, truck and trailer stay paired — no rebuilding the same trio every load.
  • One menu per concept — the same data never appears under two different names.
  • A working view out of the box instead of a 50-column table you have to assemble.
Phase 2 · 2 directions · by end of December

Loads stop being typed by hand

What you get
  • One inbox for every offer — boards, broker emails, tenders, pasted text.
  • Scored on arrival against the trucks you actually have free.
  • Rate confirmations read themselves into a load. The dispatcher only checks.
  • Delivery paperwork verified before it goes to the factoring company.
The problem today

A dispatcher works six browser tabs and retypes the same numbers into a form. Every load costs minutes that produce nothing.

Multiply the minutes per load by loads per month — that figure is what this phase takes back.

Where we go further
  • The same load on three boards collapses to one card, not three you compare by hand.
  • Document reading is in the base product — comparable add-ons elsewhere run $49–$399 a month.
  • Paperwork checked before factoring — this is the thing that stops payments being held.
  • Scoring uses your real capacity, not a generic match — empty miles, hours left, expected margin.
Phase 3 · 4 directions · by early February

The money side closes

What you get
  • Invoices from delivered loads, batched, routed to factoring or the customer.
  • Driver pay calculated automatically — deductions, advances and loans included.
  • Vendor bills and QuickBooks, so your accountant keeps their tools.
  • The driver's phone app — check-ins, photos, paperwork, works offline.
  • Live tracking on the map.
The problem today

Two things are manual at once: the money math, and knowing what actually happened on the road. Somebody phones a driver to ask if he unloaded.

After this phase the driver's check-in moves the load, and the settlement builds itself from trips that really closed.

Where we go further
  • One batch mechanism built once and reused for invoices, driver pay and vendor bills — identical behaviour everywhere.
  • Undoing a posted batch is its own permission, not a side effect of edit rights.
  • Offline-first driver app. Drivers lose signal constantly; nothing is lost when they do.
Phase 4 · 4 directions · by late March

The outside world connects

What you get
  • Fuel cards and tolls arrive on their own and attach to the right truck and driver.
  • Load boards feed straight into your inbox of offers.
  • Profit by customer, lane, driver and truck.
  • True cost per mile, with fuel and tolls counted.
  • Automatic status updates to brokers — they stop calling to ask.
  • Fuel tax by state, from miles already recorded.
The problem today

You can see revenue. You cannot easily see profit — because fuel, tolls and repairs live in other people's statements and get typed in later, if at all.

This is the phase that separates "we are busy" from "we are making money on this lane".

Where we go further
  • One connection framework per category. Adding a provider that is not on the list is a small job, not a project.
  • Unmatched transactions get assigned smartly instead of piling up for someone to sort.
  • Profit by lane — the report dispatchers actually use, and the one most systems bury.
Phase 5 · 2 directions · by mid-April

Nothing expires unnoticed

What you get
  • One screen with every driver, truck and trailer against every required document.
  • Warnings ahead of time — by date or by mileage, to the person who should act.
  • Repair orders with parts and labor, approved before payment.
  • Scheduled service on a date or an odometer reading, whichever comes first.
  • Inspections, accidents and claims recorded with the cost where it belongs.
The problem today

An expired medical card is discovered at a roadside inspection — the most expensive possible moment to find out.

This phase moves that discovery weeks earlier, onto a screen someone actually looks at.

Where we go further
  • A repair cost splits across four payers — driver, the truck itself, the shop under warranty, or the customer — with your real remaining expense shown.
  • Two independent triggers for service: date and mileage, whichever comes first.
  • One shared vocabulary for deadlines across every part of the system.

Decisions we need · 1 of 2

Six questions — and what we would do

You should not have to invent answers. Here is our recommendation on each, with the reasoning. Disagree and we change it — that is the point of asking now.

Electronic tenders from large customers
Receiving load offers from retailers is straightforward. Sending statuses and invoices back the same way is a separate build.
We recommendReceive only, for now. Add the outbound half when a real customer demands it — that is roughly a week of work saved today and nothing lost.
How far the driver app goes
Check-ins, photos and paperwork are certain. Hours-of-service display and reefer temperature are extra.
We recommendShip the full check-in flow first; add hours and temperature after drivers have used it a month. Start the Apple and Google accounts in month one — approval takes longer than the code.
Drivers who own their trucks
Their pay works differently: fuel and lease come out of their settlement.
We recommendTell us now even if the answer is "maybe next year". Designing for it costs almost nothing today and is expensive to retrofit.
How far the load search should go
One scored list your dispatcher works is certain. A system that negotiates with brokers by itself is possible.
We recommendScored inbox first, and revisit automatic negotiation after three months of real use. We will not put a date on it before then.
External dashboards
Reporting is built in. Connecting an outside analytics service on top is extra work.
We recommendSkip it. Nothing you have described needs it, and the built-in reports cover profit by lane, driver, customer and truck.
Maintenance and safety — really needed?
These sit in the last phase and could be dropped to finish earlier.
We recommendKeep them. You own trucks and carry drivers; an inspection does not care which phase it was scheduled in.

Half a day with your dispatchers
