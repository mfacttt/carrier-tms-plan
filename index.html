Phase 1 · 5 areas · by mid-November

Foundation and the dispatcher's day

What you get
  • Your company set up once — legal entities, logins, who sees what, how pay is calculated.
  • All your data in one place — customers, trucks, trailers, drivers, yards.
  • Loads from broker offer to "delivered," with profit tracked on each one.
  • A weekly board — drag a load onto a driver.
  • A map — where everything is right now.
The problem today

Your dispatching lives inside someone else's system, on someone else's terms, at someone else's price. Your operating data — every load, rate and route you've ever run — sits in someone else's database.

After this phase it's yours, and the history you build up becomes an asset instead of leverage held over you.

Where we go further
  • Profit shows the moment a load is entered, not at month's end.
  • Driver, truck and trailer stay paired together — no rebuilding the same trio for every load.
  • One menu per concept — the same data never shows up under two different names.
  • A working view out of the box, not a 50-column table you have to build yourself.
Phase 2 · 2 areas · by end of December

Loads stop being typed by hand

Where loads come from: manual entry (Phase 1), pasted email text (Phase 2), reading a rate confirmation PDF (Phase 2), a load board offer pasted in by hand (Phase 2), automatic load board connections via API (Phase 4), EDI tenders from large customers (Phase 4).

What you get
  • One inbox for every offer — load boards, broker emails, tenders, pasted text.
  • Instant scoring against the trucks you actually have free.
  • Rate confirmations read themselves into the load. The dispatcher just checks it.
  • Delivery paperwork gets verified before it goes to the factoring company.
The problem today

Your dispatcher works across six browser tabs, retyping the same numbers into a form. Every load costs minutes that produce nothing.

Multiply minutes per load by loads per month — that number is exactly what this phase gives back.

Where we go further
  • The same load posted on three boards collapses into one card, not three to compare by hand.
  • Document reading is part of the base product — comparable add-ons elsewhere run $49–$399/month.
  • Verifying paperwork before factoring is exactly what stops payment delays.
  • Scoring uses your real capacity, not a generic matching algorithm — deadhead miles, hours left, expected margin.
Phase 3 · 4 areas · by early February

The money side closes itself out

What you get
  • Invoices from delivered loads, batched together, sent to factoring or the customer.
  • Driver pay calculated automatically — deductions, advances and loans included.
  • Vendor bills and QuickBooks, so your bookkeeper keeps the tools they already use.
  • A driver mobile app — check-ins, photos, documents, works offline.
  • Live tracking on the map.
The problem today

Two things get done by hand at once: calculating the money, and figuring out what actually happened on the road. Someone calls the driver to ask if they unloaded yet.

After this phase a driver check-in moves the load, and pay assembles itself from trips that actually closed out.

Where we go further
  • One batch-processing engine, built once and reused for invoices, driver pay and vendor bills — the same behavior everywhere.
  • Reversing a posted batch is its own permission, not a side effect of edit access.
  • The driver app is offline-first. Drivers lose signal constantly; nothing gets lost when they do.
Phase 4 · 4 areas · by end of March

The outside world connects

What you get
  • Fuel and toll charges arrive on their own and attach to the right truck and driver.
  • Load boards feed your offer inbox directly.
  • Profit by customer, route, driver and truck.
  • Real cost per mile, fuel and tolls included.
  • Automatic status updates to brokers — they stop calling to ask.
  • Fuel tax by state, from miles already on record.
The problem today

You can see revenue. You can't see profit nearly as easily — because fuel, tolls and repairs sit in someone else's statements and get entered later, if at all.

This is the phase that separates "we're busy" from "we're making money on this route."

Where we go further
  • One connection framework per category. Adding a provider that isn't on the list is a small task, not a project.
  • Unmatched transactions get routed intelligently, instead of piling up for manual sorting.
  • Profit by route — a report dispatchers actually use, and one most systems hide.
Phase 5 · 2 areas · by mid-April

Nothing expires unnoticed

What you get
  • One screen with every driver, truck and trailer against every document they need.
  • Advance warnings — by date or by mileage, sent to the right person.
  • Repair orders with parts and labor, approved before payment.
  • Scheduled maintenance by date or odometer reading, whichever comes first.
  • Inspections, accidents and claims logged with cost attached where it belongs.
The problem today

An expired medical card gets discovered at a roadside inspection — the most expensive possible moment to find out.

This phase moves that discovery weeks earlier, onto a screen someone is actually watching.

Where we go further
  • Repair cost splits across four payers — driver, the truck itself, a warranty shop or the customer — showing your real remaining cost.
  • Two independent triggers for maintenance: date and mileage, whichever comes first.
  • One shared deadline vocabulary across every part of the system.
Across the whole product

Where we're deliberately doing it differently

Not promises — decisions already made in the plan, each with its own reason.

Cost

Nothing important sits behind a paywall

Document reading, broker status updates and chat are part of the base product. Elsewhere these are separate monthly add-ons at $49–$399 each.

Clarity

One menu per concept

The same records never show up under two different names in two places. Less to learn, fewer chances to get it wrong.

Usability

A strong default view

Instead of a 50-column table you have to configure, you get a view that works right away — and you can save your own on top of it.

Engineering

Built once, reused everywhere

Batch processing, permissions, deadline tracking — each built once and used everywhere, so behavior stays consistent and changes stay cheap.

Flexibility

Connections are a template

One framework per category means the next fuel card or telematics provider is days of work, not a new project.

Ownership

This is your system

Your code, your data, your roadmap. Nothing about how you operate is dictated by a vendor's pricing page.

Decisions we need from you

Six questions about integrations

The answers decide which outside services we connect to, and when to start requesting access.

01
Do you need live GPS tracking?
If you already have GPS or telematics — which system, so we connect to it instead.
02
Which fuel cards do you use?
For example WEX, Comdata, EFS, Fuelman, TCH, Fleetcor — or your own.
03
Do you keep your books in QuickBooks?
If so — which version (Online or Desktop), so invoices and payments land there on their own.
04
Do you have EDI partners today?
Exactly who you already exchange tenders with by EDI — their format becomes the first one we build.
05
Do you use factoring?
If so — which company (they pay you on an invoice right away, instead of waiting on the broker, for a small fee).
06
Which load boards do you take offers from?
For example DAT, Truckstop, Sylectus — we build auto-connections for those.

Getting started

Three things, in this order

Week one

Your answers

Six decisions from the previous slides. They set the final scope, and we've already recommended an answer for most of them.

Week one — don't delay this

Access paperwork

Fuel cards, factoring, load boards, QuickBooks. Setting these up takes two to six months and it's the single most common reason a project like this slips. It isn't in our hands.

Then

Half a day with your dispatchers

We watch how the work actually gets done today. Everything we build is shaped by that morning.

Then the clock starts

Phase 1 begins. Roughly two months in, you're already dispatching on your own system — and every phase after that builds on something you already use every day.

What we ask of you during the project

Thirty minutes every two weeks. Look at what we built, and object early. A complaint in week four is a conversation; the same complaint in month five is a rebuild.
